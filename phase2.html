Ideation & Evaluation

Four digital solutions — two human-generated, two AI-generated — systematically evaluated against strategic criteria to identify the optimal intervention for Unite Students.

Introduction

From Insight to Action

Phase 1 provided evidence that despite still being competent within their sector, Unite Students has considerable risk of jeopardizing their operations due to their increasing utility expenses (currently representing the 2nd highest line item at £30.5 million). Their preferred method of billing for all services is creating “moral hazard” conditions for tenants where they overconsume energy and water. This is partially responsible for abating total accounting return in 2025 by 4% (from ~7.9% to ~4.0%).

In Phase 2, we transition from identifying systemic issues to generating a solution that will balance students’ need for simplicity with their urgent need for improved operational efficiency. The next section explores how digital interventions could restore consumption signals using behavioral economics and incentivize students to consume in a way that is aligned with Unite’s cost efficiency and ESG objectives, which reduces the risk of a “brown discount” that threatens property values as well as greater interest rates on loans.

Solution 1: IoT-Driven “FloodGuard” Predictive Risk Platform

Key Features: This involves the deployment of a portfolio-wide IoT sensor network integrated into a centralized AI-driven dashboard to monitor real-time environmental data. “FloodGuard” would use predictive algorithms to identify properties at risk before flooding occurs. It moves flood mitigation from manual oversight to an automated early-warning system that triggers preventative maintenance protocols, such as automated flood barriers or pump activation.

Benefits:
• Financial: Directly mitigates the projected £37.8 million in potential flooding damage costs by 2050 and assists in securing lower insurance premiums through evidenced proactive risk management.
• Operational: Reduces the burden on property teams by replacing manual “human supervision” with automated alerts, closing the technological gap with competitors like GSA.

Business Challenges: This initiative addresses Climate Change Risks, specifically the tripling of assets at high risk of flooding under extreme climate scenarios.

Key Data: The number of assets at high risk of flooding is projected to rise from 23 to 82 by 2100. Proactive mitigation is essential to avoid the “brown discount” that devalues non-resilient properties.

Selection Rationale

Why We Selected Solution 1: Real-Time Utilities Tracking & Rewards

Solution 1 scored 17/20 — the highest across all four criteria — demonstrating the strongest alignment with Unite Students' strategic priorities, the clearest path to measurable ROI, and the greatest potential for customer value creation.

Unite Students’ bills-included pricing model, while highly valued by residents, has created a structural moral hazard. With zero marginal cost for additional energy and water consumption, students are incentivised to over-consume utilities in order to “recoup” perceived value. This behavioural distortion has become financially material: fixed utility costs are now Unite’s second-largest expense, rising 13% annually to £30.5 million, contributing to a decline in total accounting returns from 7.9% to 4.0%.

At the same time, student demand signals strongly support a digital intervention rather than the removal of the bills-included model. While 85% of students prioritise bills-included accommodation for budget certainty, 69% also want greater control over their energy usage, and 58% actively consider sustainability features when choosing accommodation. This highlights a clear opportunity to correct consumption behaviour without undermining the value proposition students care most about.

Academic research further validates this approach, showing that students are most likely to adopt sustainable behaviours when real-time feedback is paired with visible monetary rewards, such as vouchers. Embedding real-time utilities tracking and a tiered, gamified rewards system within Unite’s resident app directly leverages these behavioural mechanisms, reintroducing a consumption signal while maintaining pricing simplicity.

Strategically, the initiative also mitigates competitive and asset-level risks. Competitors are already deploying automated energy monitoring systems, while Unite’s reliance on human supervision leaves it exposed to rising energy intensity, margin pressure, and potential “brown discount” effects on property valuation. By aligning student incentives with Unite’s cost efficiency and ESG objectives, this solution delivers measurable financial, operational, and sustainability benefits through a single, scalable digital enhancement.
