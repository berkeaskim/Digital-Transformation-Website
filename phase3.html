Digital Solution Explanation

The Saver Plan & Rewards Dashboard

Overview of the Saver Plan & Rewards Dashboard

Our proposed solution is a real-time energy and water consumption tracking technology, which would be integrated into the current Unite Students app.

Currently, utility costs represent a significant financial challenge for Unite Students, rising by 13% in the past year alone to £30.5 million and contributing to their second-largest expenditure. This is a major factor in a halving of the 2025 total accounting returns from 7.9% to 4.0%. Without intervention, properties at risk of overheating due to utility abuse are projected to triple from 44 to 128 by 2050, risking £15 million in annual income. However, the traditional industry model of fixed, all-inclusive rent actively incentivises the students to overconsume utilities in an attempt to “recoup” perceived costs already paid. This structural issue necessitates a behavioural rather than purely technical solution.

Strategic Rationale

Research by Ivanova et al. (2025) confirms that students are most likely to adopt sustainable behaviours when real-time feedback is paired with visible monetary rewards like vouchers. Accordingly, our solution aims to use external rewards and social pressure to encourage students to be more conscious about their usage of electricity and water, while helping Unite to reduce its fixed operational costs.

The solution will involve using gadgets to track utilities in individual bedrooms, for instance, measuring the amount of time a light or a heater is switched on, or a daily total pressure gauge reading for water taps. Such technologies are already widely deployed in domestic and commercial contexts, and utility providers themselves routinely collect similar data.

How It Works

Unite will establish a two-tier standard for measuring student utility access. Firstly, industry and internal data will be used to calculate the average utility usage across student accommodations. However, as current averages are likely inflated due to widespread overuse, Unite would need to set a “bar” they deem “reasonable” for students, for instance, 20% below the industry average. Secondly, Unite would collect updated consumption data. If the weekly usage average falls below the existing standard, this lower level would be adopted as the new “bar”, preventing standards from becoming outdated or unambitious.

From the student perspective, the app would include a real-time reflection of energy and water consumption in the bedroom, alongside a simple summary of recent usage trends (by days, weeks or months), comparing it directly against the “bar” and raising alerts when you rise above it. By extension, students can earn points based on the percentage and how consistently they stay below the “bar”. Said points can be redeemed as vouchers with Unite’s partners, such as supermarket discounts, Unite renewal discounts or even cheaper plane ticket bookings. Additionally, anonymised competitive features could be introduced, allowing students to compare their performance with other rooms on the same floor. Collective rewards, such as bonus points for the most efficient floor, would drive peer pressure to more sustained reductions in consumption.

AI Component

What is the AI aspect?

Artificial intelligence and machine learning would be embedded within the app to analyse consumption patterns, generate alerts for both students and facilities staff, and produce short-term forecasts of individual utility usage. These projections could also estimate potential loyalty points, providing students with forward-looking incentives to adjust their behaviour proactively rather than reactively.

Ethical Concerns & Mitigations

The only disadvantage of this digital method is the privacy concern, which is that the students will be closely monitored even in private spaces. However, to alleviate this, the data will be anonymised, and any data related to a particular room will be deleted at the end of the month, leaving only the weekly floor averages. Unite will adhere to the GDPR fully and will not sell the data to any third party.

A further limitation relates to the perceived value of rewards. If vouchers are viewed as insufficiently attractive, students may be unwilling to modify their behaviour. To mitigate this, vouchers can be specifically tailored to frequent student needs, for instance, supermarket discounts or free laundry credits, things that students have to use every week. For further motivation, Unite could host a celebration of achievements, for example, a pizza party whenever a floor stays below the “bar” for an entire month, turning utility savings from individual decisions to collective social pressure.

Value to Customers / End-Users

Beyond the immediate savings on the £30.5 million utility bill, this solution acts as a preventative maintenance tool. This will allow Unite to identify any anomalies that could indicate faulty hardware, such as a leaky pipe or a faulty heater, and stop the problem before any expensive damage is incurred. Additionally, given the increasing focus of institutional investors on ESG considerations, this will also position Unite as a market leader in green student accommodation, which could lower the cost of future capital.
